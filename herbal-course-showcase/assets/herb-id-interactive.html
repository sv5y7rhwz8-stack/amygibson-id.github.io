Herbal Identification & First Aid

Field Guide to Distinguishing Traits

Objective: identify common medicinal herbs using distinguishing characteristics such as leaf shape, flower structure, habitat, and growth pattern.

Elderberry
Sambucus nigra / canadensis
Leaf Shape
Compound, pinnate leaves with 5–9 pointed, saw-toothed leaflets arranged in opposite pairs along a central stalk.
Flower Structure
Broad, flat-topped clusters (umbels) of tiny, five-petaled creamy-white flowers, later forming drooping clusters of dark purple-black berries.
Habitat
Moist soil along stream banks, forest edges, ditches, and hedgerows; full sun to partial shade.
Growth Pattern
Fast-growing, multi-stemmed shrub or small tree, 6–12 feet tall, with arching woody branches.
Dandelion
Taraxacum officinale
Leaf Shape
Deeply toothed, lance-shaped leaves forming a low basal rosette; jagged teeth point back toward the base — the "lion's tooth" that gives the plant its name.
Flower Structure
Single bright yellow composite flower head of tightly packed ray florets, atop a hollow, leafless stem; matures into a spherical white seed puff.
Habitat
Highly adaptable — lawns, meadows, roadsides, and disturbed soil, in full sun.
Growth Pattern
Low-growing perennial rosette with a deep taproot; no true branching stem, just leaves hugging the ground.
Echinacea
Echinacea purpurea
Leaf Shape
Coarse, rough-textured, lance-to-oval leaves with prominent veins, arranged alternately up a sturdy stem.
Flower Structure
Large daisy-like flower with drooping pink-purple petals surrounding a raised, spiny, cone-shaped orange-brown center.
Habitat
Dry prairies, open woodlands, and rocky glades in full sun; drought-tolerant once established.
Growth Pattern
Upright, clump-forming perennial, 2–4 feet tall, with sturdy branching stems.
